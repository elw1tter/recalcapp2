    Where my finance interest started

    When I was young, my dad started a small industrial internet-of-things company. Growing up, he talked with me about the operational side of it, which I found genuinely interesting long before I knew exactly what finance was.

    Introduction to depth

    In high school I watched closey as the company neared financial distress; revenue problems, spending that had not been sustainable, and short-term borrowing to fund operations becoming nearly impossible almost overnight.

    This introduced me to the depth and complexity of finance, something that I knew I wanted to build on going into college.

    Who I am outside of academics

    My family is originally from the Indianapolis area but moved to the Denver area just over two years ago, so proximity to a big city is part of what drew me to Boston College.

    When I am home I mountain bike, hike, ski, and play piano. In Boston I spend most of my free time exploring the city and looking for new places to eat.

    Respite care provider

    The summer before college I worked with families providing respite care to individuals with special needs. It was my first real experience of a fast-paced, team-based environment where the work changed hour to hour, and it is something I looked for more of once I got to campus.

    Coursework

    Financial Accounting (A), Fundamentals of Finance (A), Corporate Finance (in progress), Business Statistical Analysis (A). GPA 3.81, Dean’s List First Honors every semester.

    Finance major, philosophy minor. The second one is there because I like arguments that have to hold up under pressure.

    BC Investment Club — Industrials analyst

    Working in a group of analysts, I have gotten more experience applying an analytical approach in a team environment, something that has deepened my interest in investment banking specifically as that analytical team based work is central.

    Current Projects

    I am on a team building a buy pitch for Hubbell. Our main thesis revolves around utility based demand growing under structural demand drivers and recent portfolio discipline-based divestures and acquisitions.

    BC Invsetment Club General Member Stock Pitch Competition

    I pitched Generac, a back-up generator manufacturer with significant residential and commercial/industrial applications. My argument was that weather-driven cyclicality was overshadowing aging grid infrastructure and rising instability — a structural demand driver the market was treating as a one-off.

    What it taught me

    Every qualitative claim had to be quantifiable and defensible. Each thesis point needed a specific growth rate or margin over a specified horizon, or it wouldn't hold up under pressure. The analytical pressure that applies is something that attracted me to the process the most.

    I placed first out of more than 150 competitors and became an analyst as a result.

    Bankruptcy Research Assistant

    I assisted with bankruptcy research under Professor Edith Hotchkiss, working through documents from hundreds of cases. I also worked to implement LLM use to streamline the retrieval process and improve pace and accuracy.

    Reading past the task

    The job was mostly document retrieval, but oftentimes I would read the some of the filings themselves. For example, sometimes I would go into a conversion order to understand why a company had moved from Chapter 11 to Chapter 7, when the actual required task was just to record that it had.

    BC Credit & Restructuring Club

    Through weekly lectures, case studies, and a credit pitch competition, I found an interest in an area I previously knew almost nothing about — and one that turned out to be where my dad’s company’s problems actually lived.

    The Lumen short

    I pitched a short on a Lumen parent-company senior unsecured note, arguing that a prior divestiture whose proceeds funded a substantial refinancing had materially worsened that particular note’s position — even as it improved the company’s debt profile as a whole on paper.

    What I am following now

    MultiColor, a prime label manufacturer that went through a prepackaged Chapter 11 after cost inflation, industry destocking, and unsustainable leverage.

    One thing in particular I learned was how central valuation can be to these kinds of deals, as Evercore's valuation of first lien collateral was the difference between an identical claim recovering ~90 cents vs. ~3 cents.

    Failure

    First semester of my freshman year, a poor BC Investment Club interview resulted in me not getting into the analyst program. Because I wanted to go deeper into finance interest at the time, I felt this was a major setback.

    Growth under consistency

    My response to this scenario displays a key part of who I am as a worker. I stayed involved as a general member, absorbed feedback from analysts and observed pitches, and worked to actually grow as a result. Once the general member stock pitch competition came along, I was able to display how much I had grown with this mindset, winning the competition and becoming an analyst.
